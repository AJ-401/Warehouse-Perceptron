AI Video Intelligence for Warehouse Handling
An AI-Powered Field Intelligence Assistant for Safer, Damage-Free Warehouse Operations
VISION
This challenge is about creating an AI-powered Video Intelligence platform for warehouse operations that helps reduce product damage, improve material-handling discipline, and create safer loading and unloading practices.
Warehouses handle thousands of products every day through activities such as vehicle loading, unloading, movement, stacking, pallet handling, and material transfer. Many product damages occur not because of equipment failure, but because of inappropriate handling behaviour — dropping, dragging, improper stacking, excessive force, unsafe movement, incorrect pallet placement, or handling products in ways that violate established operating practices.
The challenge invites participants to build an AI + Computer Vision-powered Field Intelligence App that transforms existing warehouse cameras or smartphone cameras into an intelligent operational assistant.
The system should observe loading and unloading activities, identify potentially damaging or unsafe behaviours, provide real-time or near-real-time alerts, and generate actionable insights that can help warehouse teams prevent damage rather than investigate it after the fact.
CHALLENGE OVERVIEW
Traditional CCTV systems primarily record what happened.
The next generation of warehouse intelligence should understand what is happening.
Participants are challenged to develop an AI-powered video monitoring solution capable of observing warehouse loading and unloading activities and identifying behaviours that could result in:
Product damage
Improper handling
Dropping or impact
Incorrect stacking
Unsafe movement of material
Improper use of pallets or handling equipment
Overloading or unstable loading
Rough handling during loading/unloading
Potential damage-causing operator behaviour
The objective is not simply to monitor people.
The objective is to understand behaviour and prevent damage.
CHALLENGE STATEMENT
How might we create an AI-powered video intelligence platform that can observe warehouse loading and unloading activities, identify damage-causing behaviours, and provide timely insights that help employees handle products correctly and minimize damage?
Participants should build a native or web application that can analyse video footage from a warehouse environment and act as an AI Field Intelligence Assistant for material handling. The proposed solution should demonstrate the evolution from conventional surveillance to intelligent operational assistance.
Traditional CCTV
Camera → Recording → Human review → Incident discovered → Corrective action
AI Field Intelligence
Camera → AI perception → Behaviour understanding → Risk detection → Alert → Intervention → Learning
The emphasis should therefore be on proactive prevention rather than retrospective monitoring.

AI Behaviour Intelligence
A strong solution should move beyond simple object detection.
For example:
Basic Computer Vision
Person + box detected.
Behaviour Intelligence
Operator picks up product → moves product → product is dropped → impact event detected → product remains stationary → potential damage event identified.
The system should ideally understand the sequence of actions.
This is an important part of the challenge.
Participants should demonstrate how their solution combines:
Object Detection + Object Tracking + Action Recognition + Temporal Reasoning + Risk Classification
to understand warehouse behaviour.

Teams are encouraged to introduce a simple risk score.
For example:
Low Risk → Medium Risk → High Risk → Critical Risk
The risk score could consider:
Type of behaviour
Product involved
Height of drop
Approximate impact
Duration of improper handling
Stacking configuration
Frequency of repeated behaviour
Location
Historical incident patterns
Example:
HIGH RISK EVENTProduct dropped from approximately 1 metre during unloading.Product remained stationary after impact.Recommended action: inspect product and review unloading practice.
The objective is not to claim that damage definitely occurred unless the system has sufficient evidence.
It should distinguish between:
Observed behaviour → Potential risk → Confirmed damage
This distinction is important for responsible AI deployment.
Parameters for detecting Good/Bad Practices
Problem / Bad Practice
Good Behaviour / Expected Practice
Improper stacking – smaller packets placed below larger packets
Stack larger and heavier packets at the bottom and smaller/lighter packets on top. Ensure the complete packet is supported.
Heavy cartons/products kept on top of lighter packets
Keep heavy products at the bottom and avoid putting excessive load on lighter or fragile packages.
Throwing or dropping cartons/products
Lift and place products gently. Never throw or drop a package while loading or unloading.
Dragging cartons or KD packets
Use a trolley, pallet truck or suitable handling equipment instead of dragging products on the floor.
Rolling cartons or mattresses
Carry or move products using the appropriate material-handling equipment; do not roll products unless specifically designed for it.
Using packaging straps to lift or pull cartons
Handle the carton using proper lifting points/equipment. Do not use packaging straps as handles unless specifically designed for lifting.
Stepping or standing on cartons
Never step, stand or walk on packages. Keep a clear working path around the material.
Vertical product kept horizontally
Follow the specified product orientation and handling labels/arrows throughout storage, movement and loading.
Product larger than the pallet / inadequate pallet support
Use the correct-size pallet or trolley so that the entire product is properly supported without unsafe overhang.
Gap between dock and vehicle / uneven dock level
Use a proper dock leveller or bridge arrangement and ensure a safe, level transition before moving material.
Handling/dragging products on a wet floor
Keep the loading area dry and obstruction-free. Stop material movement until unsafe floor conditions are corrected.
Improper loading causing packets to fall
Load products in a stable and planned sequence and ensure they cannot slide, tilt or fall during handling or transport.
Mishandling large/heavy products by manually picking and dropping
Use team lifting or suitable mechanical handling equipment and place the product in a controlled manner.
Using a pallet where a trolley/material-handling device is required
Use the correct equipment for movement, such as an appropriate trolley or pallet truck.
Rough handling during loading/unloading
Handle every product carefully and in a controlled manner, particularly at transfer points.
PDI-required products being repaired during loading
Complete PDI, inspection and required repairs before loading in the designated process/work area.
Product positioned in a manner prone to bending
Position and support products correctly so that there is no unsupported span, bending or deformation.
Mattresses/products thrown on top of one another
Place products one at a time in the correct position and maintain proper stacking/support.
Random/improper staging before loading
Stage products systematically according to size, weight, sequence and vehicle-loading plan.
AI Warehouse Assistant
Participants are encouraged to add a conversational AI layer.
A supervisor could ask:
"Show me all high-risk handling events from today's unloading."
or:
"What were the three most common risky behaviours during the morning shift?"
or:
"Which loading bay had the highest number of risky events?"
or:
"Why was this event classified as high risk?"
The AI assistant should respond using the events detected by the computer-vision system rather than inventing information.

The central impact of the solution should be framed around prevention.

"We detected 25 damaged products."

"We identified 40 high-risk handling events and enabled corrective intervention before damage occurred."
This creates a shift from:
Damage Detection → Damage Prevention
and from:
CCTV Surveillance → Operational Intelligence

Responsible AI Considerations
Because the solution analyses employee behaviour, teams should demonstrate responsible use of AI.
The system should focus on process improvement and damage prevention, rather than indiscriminate employee surveillance.
Participants should consider:
Privacy
Employee consent and transparency
Data minimization
Secure video storage
Role-based access
Appropriate retention periods
Human review of significant incidents
False-positive management
Explainability of alerts
Avoiding automated punitive decisions
Bias and accuracy across different operating conditions
A strong solution should answer:
"How do we use AI to improve behaviour without turning the warehouse into a surveillance environment?"
